The Subject

A factory cook's secret recipes, recognised by Michelin

Auntie Gaik Lean's Old School Eatery is named for Beh Gaik Lean, a fourth-generation Nyonya who taught herself to cook from her mother — grinding chilli for sambal at twelve, later cooking in the staff cafeterias of Motorola, Intel, Dell and Seagate. In 2013 her eldest son, Adrian Tan, bought a 150-year-old former B.P. de Silva jewellery shophouse on Bishop Street and named the restaurant after her without telling her, coaxing her back to Penang with the line: "Don't die taking all your recipes with you."

The room leans into its name — vintage trinkets, a slow ceiling fan, 1960s music. The kitchen makes almost everything from scratch: a pie tee filling built by hand, and a gulai tumis whose paste folds in eight-plus spices including fresh saffron. The menu is, unusually, entirely pork-free.

In December 2022, at the inaugural MICHELIN Guide Kuala Lumpur & Penang, the eatery won one star — making Gaik Lean the first Malaysian woman to earn a Michelin star, and the restaurant the first Peranakan establishment in Malaysia to be so recognised (the second in the world, after Singapore's Candlenut). It has held the star every year since.

Recognition Timeline

From shophouse opening to four-year star

2013

The eatery opens on Bishop Street

Adrian Tan buys a 150-year-old former B.P. de Silva jewellery shophouse and opens the restaurant to showcase his mother's Nyonya cooking.

13 December 2022 · Guide 2023

★ Awarded its first Michelin Star

One of just four one-star restaurants in the inaugural MICHELIN Guide Kuala Lumpur & Penang. Beh Gaik Lean, then 69, becomes the first Malaysian woman to earn a star. It enters the guide directly at one star — never a Bib Gourmand.

Nov 2023 · Guide 2024

★ Star retained — and a redesign

Star held in the second edition. Local studio Nevermore reworks the interior over 20 days, introducing a royal-blue palette nodding to its Peranakan heritage (featured in Tatler Asia).

2024

Named to Tatler's Asia's Most Influential

Tatler lists Gaik Lean among Asia's most influential people of 2024, "the first Malaysian woman to be awarded a Michelin star for her soulful Peranakan plates."

Nov 2024 · Guide 2025

★ Star retained (third year)

Holds its star as the guide expands; profiled by The Star as a Michelin spot that "deserves its star" for humble, from-scratch authenticity.

Nov 2025 · Guide 2026

★ Star retained (fourth year)

Among eight one-star restaurants in the 2026 selection as Penang reinforces its "food paradise" status with 74 guide entries.

The verdict

A beloved, authentically Peranakan, family-run institution whose Curry Kapitan and Nasi Ulam are repeat standouts and whose warm, host-led hospitality drives a large body of five-star reviews. The drag on its scores comes from a consistent minority who feel it doesn't justify the Michelin star — citing inconsistency, steep prices, a non-refundable deposit, difficult reservations and, occasionally, cool service.

Methodology

Compiled from open-web research across review platforms, food blogs, news outlets, the official MICHELIN Guide and social profiles (June 2026). Quantitative review analysis pools ~28 individual reviews carrying explicit detail, alongside platform-level averages as displayed by each source. All quotes are verbatim from cited sources; none were invented.

Some platforms (Michelin, TripAdvisor, Yelp, Facebook, Instagram, NST, Vulcan Post) block automated reading; their figures and content were captured via search-result snippets and aggregators (e.g. Wanderlog) and may vary slightly from live values. TripAdvisor's score is reported variably as 4.2–4.5 across sources; 4.5/289 is used here.
